Among the metrics listed above, the "CPUUsage" is very helpful for checking your jobs' performance and efficiency.

"CPUUsage" = CPUUser/CPUhours, is the rate of your job's real usage of CPU time, divided by the total CPUhours, including idle and working time. It is a good estimate of your jobs' efficiency on the cluster.

It should be very close to 1. Otherwise, if it is less than 1 a lot (e.g., <0.9, etc.), then it is very probably that your jobs under-utilized the resources they requested.

There could be many reasons.

Please check your job settings, such as:

- MPI job configurations
- Ask for fewer resources (nodes, CPU cores, etc.) when appropriate
- Submit jobs to a shared queue instead of asking for a whole node
- Double check if you are over-running the nodes with a huge number of processes due to multiple threading
- Check if there are heavy disk I/O operations from your jobs

Also, if possible, combining many small jobs into a much bigger job can greatly improve efficiency by avoiding the overhead from the OS and Slurm.

"CPUhours": CPU time in hours used (Elapsed time * CPU count) by your job.

"CPUSYST": The CPU hours used by the operating system and related system activity. If it is high, you may need to consider checking if something is over-running the node. The smaller it is, the better.

"CPUUSER": The CPU hours used by your job's real computation. The bigger it is, the better.

"MemUsed": The maximum memory used by the job.

Due to our current setting, it sometimes can be accounted repeatedly for the same memory usage, which in turn can show a number larger than the requested memory. This often happens to multiple-threading jobs.

The rough estimate for the corrected MemUsed in this case can be obtained by:

MemUsed / nCPUs

This issue will be fixed later when we have a chance to upgrade Slurm.
